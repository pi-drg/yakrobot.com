## SIG launch (separate from the workshop)

**New SIG: Distributed Robotics Research Group**

Distributed robot coordination is a protocol problem. AI agents have started interacting with the physical world, and few people are studying the interaction protocols emerging between those worlds, mediated by blockchains. This group sits at that intersection: AI agents, blockchain, and robotics.

Building a single robot is an engineering challenge. Two or more robots produce a new set of interaction challenges: identity, payment, attestation, transport, arbitration, graceful failure.

Protocols are engineered arguments. This group is built around that idea: we argue about robot coordination protocols, and engineer to test the arguments — not just theorize and debate, but test with real robots and real experiments.

The mandate is broad: find the protocols necessary for robot coordination, then learn how to test them on real hardware. Over time, the aim is to shift the focus from engineering single useful robots to engineering arguments between multiple robots.

At launch, Yak Robotics Garage (active since 2021 in the Yak Collective) brings `yakrover-protocols` as an opinionated sub-project — a first concrete stack to argue with. We're actively looking for additional robotics initiatives to join.

**Topics to start with:** identity (ERC-8004), payment (x402), attestation (EAS, KZG, Bulletproofs), transport (libp2p, Zenoh, MQTT), coordination (auctions, contract nets, LLM-augmented planners), and precedents (Bristlemouth, swarm robotics classics).

**Two modes, two venues:**
- **Arguments** — biweekly sessions in `#distributed-robotics` on the Protocol Institute Discord. Papers, design critique, debate.
- **Engineering** — hands-on tinkering in `#yak-rover` on the Yak Collective Discord, plus the YakRoboticsGarage GitHub.
